What is an EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the fixed amount you pay your bank or lender every month until a loan is fully repaid. Each EMI has two parts: a share that repays the money you borrowed (the principal) and a share that pays the lender's interest. The total of the two stays the same every month, which makes a loan easy to plan around.

The split inside the EMI changes over time. At the start the outstanding balance is large, so most of the EMI goes towards interest. As the balance falls, interest takes less and principal takes more. For a ₹10,00,000 loan at 9% a year, the first EMI of ₹20,758 is made up of ₹7,500 interest and ₹13,258 principal. By the last month almost the entire EMI is principal.

How to use this EMI calculator

Three quick steps give you the EMI, the total cost of the loan and a year-by-year breakup. Each step below shows what you will see on the calculator above.

Step 1: Enter the loan amount and interest rate

Type the amount you plan to borrow in rupees, or drag the slider. The calculator also writes the amount in lakh or crore underneath, which helps you spot a missing zero. Then enter the annual interest rate your bank has quoted. Use the rate from the sanction letter or the lender's website, not a guess.

Step 2: Choose the loan tenure

Enter the repayment period. It is in years by default. Switch to Months if your loan is quoted that way, for example 18 or 30 months. Home loans commonly run 15 to 30 years, car loans 3 to 7 years and personal loans 1 to 5 years.

Step 3: Read your EMI and the total cost

The monthly EMI appears at the top. Below it you see the principal, the total interest over the life of the loan and the total amount you will repay. The coloured bar shows what share of your total payment is interest. Use Copy result to paste the summary into a message or a spreadsheet.

Optional: open the year-by-year schedule

Select Show year-by-year schedule to see how much principal and interest you pay in each year and how much of the loan is still outstanding at the end of it. It is useful when you plan a part-payment or want to know the balance after a few years.

EMI formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

Here P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly instalments. If the interest rate is 0%, the formula is simply P ÷ n.

Worked example: P = ₹10,00,000, annual rate 9%, tenure 5 years. Then r = 9 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0075 and n = 60. The factor (1.0075)60 is about 1.5657, so EMI = 10,00,000 × 0.0075 × 1.5657 ÷ 0.5657 = ₹20,758.36.

How tenure changes your EMI and total interest

A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises the interest you pay in total, because the balance stays outstanding for longer. The table below uses a ₹10,00,000 loan at 9%.

Stretching the loan from 5 to 20 years cuts the EMI by more than half, but the interest grows from about ₹2.5 lakh to about ₹11.6 lakh. The interest rate matters just as much. On a ₹20,00,000 loan over 15 years, moving from 7.5% to 9.5% raises the EMI from ₹18,540 to ₹20,884 and the total interest from ₹13.4 lakh to ₹17.6 lakh. Even a small difference in rate is worth negotiating.

Reducing balance and flat rate

Most banks quote home, car and education loans on a reducing balance. Interest each month is charged only on the principal still owed, which is what this calculator uses. Some lenders and dealers quote a flat rate, where interest is charged on the full original amount for the whole tenure. A flat rate looks lower but costs more. A 9% flat rate on ₹10,00,000 for 5 years means an EMI of ₹24,167, compared with ₹20,758 at 9% reducing balance. To get the same EMI as that flat loan on a reducing balance, the rate would have to be about 15.7%. When two offers use different methods, compare the EMI and the total repayment, not the quoted rate.

Frequently asked questions

How is EMI calculated?

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of months. For ₹10,00,000 at 9% for 5 years, the EMI is ₹20,758.36.

Does a longer tenure reduce my EMI?

Yes, a longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI, but you pay interest for more months, so the total interest is higher. A shorter tenure has a higher EMI and a lower total cost.

Why is most of my early EMI interest?

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is highest at the start. As you repay principal, the balance falls and so does the interest part of each EMI. The EMI itself stays the same.

What is the difference between a flat rate and a reducing balance rate?

With a reducing balance rate, interest is charged only on the principal you still owe. With a flat rate, interest is charged on the original loan amount for the entire tenure, so the same quoted rate costs much more. Compare offers by EMI and total repayment.

Will the EMI on my loan match this calculator exactly?

It should be very close for a fixed-rate loan. Your lender may add processing fees, insurance or GST on fees, and may round amounts differently. On a floating-rate loan the EMI or the tenure changes when the lender's rate changes.

Is my data sent to a server?

No. This calculator runs entirely in your browser. The amounts you enter are never uploaded or stored.
