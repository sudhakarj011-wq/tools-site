What is a discount calculator?

A discount calculator tells you how much you pay after a percentage off, how much you save, and, working the other way, what percentage discount a shop is really giving. It is useful at sales, when comparing offers, and when a seller adds an "extra 10% off" on top of the first discount.

This discount calculator has two modes. Price after discount takes the original price and one or two discounts. Discount % takes the original and sale price and tells you the percentage off.

How to use this discount calculator

Step 1: Choose what you want to find

Pick Price after discount if you know the discount and want the price. Pick Discount % if you know both prices and want the percentage.

Step 2: Enter the numbers

Type the original price and the discount. If the shop gives a second, extra discount, put it in the Extra discount box. Leave that box empty if there is none.

Step 3: Read the price and your saving

The big number is what you pay. Below it you see how much you save, the total discount as a percentage and the price after the first discount.

Step 4: Check the working

Open Show working to see each step with your numbers, so you can verify the shop's bill. Use Copy result to save a summary.

Discount formulas

Price you pay = Original price × (1 − Discount ÷ 100)
  • Amount saved = Original price − Price you pay
  • Discount % = (Original price − Sale price) ÷ Original price × 100
  • Original price from a sale price = Sale price ÷ (1 − Discount ÷ 100)

The last formula helps when you only see the discounted price. If a jacket costs ₹1,500 after 25% off, the original price was 1,500 ÷ 0.75 = ₹2,000.

Two discounts are not added together

This is the most common mistake. A "20% off, plus an extra 10% off" deal is not 30% off. The second discount is taken from the already reduced price. On ₹2,500, 20% off gives ₹2,000, and 10% off that gives ₹1,800. The total discount is ₹700, which is 28% of ₹2,500, not 30%.

The same is true for bigger numbers. "50% off, then another 50% off" is 75% off, so you pay a quarter of the price, not nothing. The order of the two discounts does not change the final price, because multiplying is the same in either order.

Tips for comparing offers

  • Compare the final price, not the percentage. A 40% discount on a higher marked price can cost more than 25% off a lower one.
  • "Buy 1 get 1 free" means 50% off each item when you buy two.
  • Check whether GST is included. If the price shown is before tax, add the tax with the GST Calculator.
  • For other percentage sums such as a percentage increase, use the Percentage Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a discount?

Multiply the original price by the discount percentage and divide by 100 to get the amount saved, then subtract it from the price. For 15% off ₹1,999, the saving is ₹299.85 and the price is ₹1,699.15.

How do I find the discount percentage from two prices?

Subtract the sale price from the original price, divide by the original price and multiply by 100. For ₹1,299 reduced to ₹999 that is 300 ÷ 1,299 × 100 = 23.09%.

How do I calculate two successive discounts?

Apply the first discount, then apply the second to the new price. 20% and then 10% on ₹2,500 gives ₹2,000 and then ₹1,800, a total discount of 28%. Enter the second discount in the Extra discount box.

How do I find the original price before a discount?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). If the price after 25% off is ₹1,500, the original price is 1,500 ÷ 0.75 = ₹2,000.

Can the discount be more than 100%?

No. A 100% discount makes the item free. This calculator accepts discounts from 0 to 100%.

Is my data sent to a server?

No. This tool runs entirely in your browser. What you enter is never uploaded or stored.
